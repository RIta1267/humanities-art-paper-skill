# Style And Revision

Use this reference for Chinese academic prose, CSSCI-style polish, abstracts, keywords, conclusions, transitions, and peer-review responses.

## Revision Order

Revise in this order:

1. Argument: central claim, section claims, evidence relation.
2. Structure: paragraph order, transitions, repetition, section titles.
3. Concepts: definition, scope, consistency.
4. Evidence: source attribution and claim strength.
5. Sentence style: clarity, rhythm, concision.

Do not polish sentences before diagnosing argument problems unless the user explicitly asks for language-only editing.

## Chinese Academic Style

Prefer:

- Clear subject and verb.
- Medium-length sentences.
- Concrete nouns tied to materials.
- Explicit logical connectors only where needed.
- Controlled theoretical vocabulary.

Avoid:

- "具有重要意义" without specifying significance.
- "在新时代背景下" as filler.
- "赋能、彰显、助推、路径优化" unless the paper is policy/application oriented.
- "多元一体、守正创新、活态传承" as decorative language.
- Excessive "本文认为" in every paragraph.

## Abstract Pattern

For humanities/art papers, use 4-5 sentences:

1. Research object and problem.
2. Materials and method.
3. Main analysis or finding.
4. Argument/contribution.
5. Optional implication or limit.

Do not make the abstract a background introduction.

## Conclusion Pattern

1. Return to the research question.
2. Summarize the answer, not the chapter list.
3. State contribution at the proper scale.
4. Name limits or future materials if useful.

Avoid introducing new evidence in the conclusion.

## Peer-Review Response

For reviewer comments:

1. Thank briefly.
2. Identify the issue precisely.
3. State the revision made.
4. Quote or summarize the changed section.
5. If disagreeing, explain with evidence and offer a limited revision.

Keep the tone respectful and concrete.
