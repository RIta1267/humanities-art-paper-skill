# Evidence And Materials

Use this reference when working with fieldwork, interviews, local gazetteers, archives, images, scores, policy texts, classroom cases, catalogues, or performance records.

## Evidence Discipline

State what each material type can and cannot prove:

- Interview: experience, memory, self-understanding, institutional position, oral history clues. It does not automatically prove historical fact.
- Fieldnotes: observed practice and context. They require date, place, participants, and observer position.
- Local gazetteer: official/local historical framing. Cross-check edition, compiler, date, and political context.
- Archive: administrative trace, event record, institutional decision, correspondence, catalog data. Explain provenance.
- Score/transcription: musical form, mode, rhythm, melodic contour, textual setting. It does not alone explain social function.
- Image/video: staging, costume, gesture, spatial relation, display logic, media framing. Avoid treating image as transparent reality.
- Policy: governance intention and official categories. It does not prove implementation.
- Database/statistics: distribution and frequency. Explain source construction and missing data.

## Triangulation

Prefer claims supported by at least two kinds of material when discussing change, causality, or historical continuity.

Examples:

- "Heritage nomination changed repertoire selection" should use policy documents, performance lists, interviews, and before/after repertoire comparison if possible.
- "A song entered school education" should use curriculum documents, teaching materials, classroom observation, teacher interviews, and student work if possible.
- "A performance style became stage-oriented" should use video/image comparison, troupe records, interviews, and audience/venue context if possible.

## Fieldwork Reporting

Include only available facts:

- Time and place.
- Interviewee identity or anonymized role.
- Collection method.
- Researcher's relation to the field.
- Limits of access, memory, translation, transcription, or selection.

Do not fabricate ethics approval, consent procedures, interview counts, or anonymization if not provided.

## Analysis Moves

Use humanities-friendly verbs:

- classify, contextualize, compare, reconstruct, interpret, trace, distinguish, problematize, periodize, locate, mediate, translate, institutionalize, stage, aestheticize, archive, canonize, teach, circulate, appropriate, negotiate.

Avoid unsupported causal verbs:

- prove, determine, inevitably lead to, fully reveal, fundamentally solve.
