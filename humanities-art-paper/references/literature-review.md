# Literature Review

Use this reference for literature maps, review sections, citation positioning, and "research status" writing.

## Review Logic

Organize by research problem, not by author list. Useful axes:

- Historical stages: early description, classification, theory-building, recent revision.
- Schools or positions: preservation, performance, institution, identity, market, education, media, heritage governance.
- Materials: archive-based, fieldwork-based, text/image/score analysis, statistical/database work.
- Methods: ethnography, historical reconstruction, formal analysis, discourse analysis, sociology of art, education research.
- Debate: what is agreed, what is disputed, what remains unasked.

## Literature Matrix

Before drafting a long review, create a matrix:

| Cluster | Representative works | Materials/method | Main contribution | Limit for this paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use only verified references supplied by the user or found through an explicit research step. If references are not verified, use placeholders such as `[Author, Year - verify]`.

## Four-Part Paragraph Pattern

1. Topic sentence: define the cluster of scholarship.
2. Representative works: cite or name the key contributions if provided by the user.
3. Evaluation: identify method, material, contribution, and limits.
4. Position: explain how the current paper enters the gap.

## Anti-Patterns

- "A said..., B said..., C said..." without comparison.
- Praising every work as "valuable" without specifying value.
- Claiming no one has studied the topic when adjacent work exists.
- Treating lack of exact title match as a research gap.
- Using vague gaps such as "not systematic enough" without saying what system is needed.

## Useful Gap Types

- Material gap: newly available archives, fieldwork, images, scores, local documents, repertory lists, classroom records.
- Scale gap: existing work discusses national discourse, but the case shows county, village, school, troupe, platform, or institution-level mechanisms.
- Method gap: prior description lacks close reading, performance analysis, institutional analysis, or comparative explanation.
- Conceptual gap: an inherited category hides internal diversity or historical change.
- Temporal gap: transformation after policy, market, platform, curriculum, urbanization, tourism, or heritage nomination has not been explained.

## Positioning Sentence

End the literature review with a sentence that states how the paper enters the debate:

"Existing studies have clarified [A], but have not yet explained [B] through [materials/method]. This paper therefore uses [case/material] to argue [claim]."

If this sentence feels forced, the review has not yet found the paper's position.

## Output Checklist

For every literature review output, include:

- 3-6 literature clusters.
- Each cluster's contribution and limitation.
- The paper's position after the review.
- A warning for any citation or source that the model cannot verify.
